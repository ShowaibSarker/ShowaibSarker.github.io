Do the unlevered test today. Put the purchase price in year zero, the cash flow before debt service in years one to five, add the net sale proceeds to year five, then run NPV and IRR. That tells you whether the building earns its keep before any financing. On our Walgreens it does not, and that is the most interesting thing in the whole model.

Step 1. The listing

What you get here

Address, asking price, building size, number of units, in-place NOI if the broker publishes it, and the lease structure.

  1. Open the listing. Ours is LoopNet, 1041 W Main St, Whitewater. Crexi carries different listings so check both.
  2. Write down the asking price, the building size and the number of suites.
  3. Look for a financial detail panel. If NOI is published, take it. If the listing says call for financials, you will build NOI yourself from rent and expenses.
  4. Find the lease type. Triple net, NN, modified gross or full service. This one line decides your operating expense ratio and your capital reserve. If it is not stated, email the listing broker and ask. Brokers answer students.
  5. Screenshot the whole listing page with the date visible. Listings come down.
Two traps. A published cap rate on a flyer is a marketing number. Recompute it yourself as NOI divided by asking price. And the owner of a net leased store is almost never the company operating it, so do not search the county owner field for the tenant's name.

Step 2. The county record

What you get here

Parcel number, owner of record, acreage, year built, assessed land value, assessed improvement value, property class, tax history and sometimes a sale history.

  1. Start with the map, not the search box. The Walworth County parcel search will not accept a street address. Open the county map or the assessor portal, find the building, click the parcel, and read off the tax key. Ours is /A 43800001.
  2. Take that key to the Accurate assessor portal and open the full record.
  3. Record the owner of record. Ours is COT WG MEADOW LLC of Deerfield, Illinois. Walgreens is only the tenant.
  4. Record the assessed land value and the assessed improvement value separately. You need the land line on its own for the cost approach.
  5. Record the most recent property tax bill. On any building that is not net leased this is your single biggest operating expense and it proves an expense ratio of zero is impossible.
  6. Record the year built. You need it for depreciation in the cost approach.
  7. Check the sale history. If the parcel has a recorded sale you can compute what the last buyer paid per square foot.
  8. Save the record as a PDF with the date.

If your property sits in a different county, every Wisconsin county runs one of two systems. Search the county name plus land records and the portal will come up. Jefferson County uses its own land records site and Washington County uses Ascent.

Step 3. The income lines

LineWhere it comes from
Market rent per sf actual On a net lease with published NOI, divide NOI by building size. Ours gives exactly $15.00 per square foot, which tells you it is the contract rent. Otherwise take asking rents from comparable listings on the same corridor and average them.
Vacancy estimate Zero only where one tenant sits on an in-place corporate lease with term remaining. Everywhere else use the submarket vacancy from a Colliers, CBRE or JLL market report for the metro. Never zero on a multi-tenant building even when it is fully leased today, because leases roll.
Operating expense ratio estimate The lease structure decides it. Triple net puts taxes, insurance and maintenance on the tenant so the owner's ratio is near zero. Gross puts them on the owner and the ratio runs 35 to 45 percent. Build it from the bottom up: start with the real property tax bill off the county record, then add insurance, management, maintenance and utilities from a benchmark such as the IREM Income and Expense Analysis. That beats grabbing one ratio from anywhere.
Capital reserve estimate Zero only where the lease puts roof and structure on the tenant. Otherwise 2 to 5 percent of EGI, or a dollar per square foot allowance. Something has to pay for the next roof.
Growth estimate If the lease has contractual rent steps, use them. Otherwise use inflation from FRED series CPIAUCSL or a market rent forecast from a broker report. Never a round number with nothing behind it.
PGI computedBuilding size times rent per square foot.

Step 4. The rates

RateWhere it comes from
Going-in cap rate actual NOI divided by asking price, computed by you. Cross check it against closed sales of similar property on Crexi and against the cap rate section of a broker market report. Ours is 6.65 percent.
Exit cap rate estimate Above the going-in rate. The building is five years older at sale and the lease has five fewer years on it, so a buyer then demands more. Twenty five to fifty basis points is a defensible widening. Say which and why.
Required return estimate Build it up. Start with the ten year Treasury from the daily par yield curve, then add premiums for illiquidity, for single tenant concentration and for leverage. Write the build-up out in your report.
Selling costs estimate Brokerage plus title plus transfer. Three percent on a net lease sale, more on a multi tenant deal.
Loan rate and LTV estimate Ask a lender for a quote if you can. Otherwise take the matching Treasury and add a commercial mortgage spread, and cite both. Sixty to seventy percent LTV is typical.

Step 5. Comps and the cost approach

Three comparable sales

  1. Search Crexi sold listings for the same property type within a reasonable radius, closed in the last two years.
  2. Pull the county sale history for each comp parcel so you have the recorded price and not the asking price.
  3. Compute price per square foot for each.
  4. Adjust each one to your property for location, size, age, condition and lease structure. Write the adjustment as a percentage and say what drove it.
  5. Average the adjusted figures and multiply by your building size.

The cost approach

  1. Land value from the assessed land line on the county record, sanity checked against vacant pad sales on the same corridor. Do not use the total assessment.
  2. Replacement cost new per square foot from a published cost manual for your building type in this region.
  3. Depreciation from effective age over total economic life. Take the year built from the county record and use a forty to fifty year life for retail.
  4. Land plus depreciated improvements is your indicated value.

When you cannot find the exact number

You will estimate. That is not a failure. Appraisers estimate every one of these. What separates a defensible estimate from a guess is three things, and all three go in your Sources tab.
  1. Name the method. Not the number. Say how you arrived at it. "Treasury plus 450 basis points" is a method. "Nine percent" is not.
  2. Name a source that a reader could open. A benchmark publication, a market report, a county record, a lender quote. Something outside your own head.
  3. Say which way it would move the answer. If your exit cap is too tight your value is too high. Tell the reader that, then show it in the sensitivity table.

Then mark the cell green in your model so anyone reading it knows at a glance which numbers are retrieved and which are judged.

What will not pass. A number with nothing beside it. A number taken from an AI tool and used as if the tool were the source. A ratio copied from a different property type. An expense ratio of zero on a building where the owner pays the taxes.

Step 6. Check yourself before you move on

  • Does your built-up NOI match the in-place NOI on the listing? If it does, your rent and expense assumptions are right. Ours matches to the dollar.
  • Does NOI divided by asking price equal the cap rate you wrote down?
  • Does asking price divided by building size give a sensible price per square foot for this market?
  • Does your reconciled value sit inside the range of your three approaches?
  • Does the county assessment land anywhere near your value conclusion? It will usually be lower. If it is wildly higher, something is wrong.
  • Is every number on the Sources tab with a link and a date?
Our demonstration property, checked. NOI $226,845 built from $15.00 per square foot on 15,123 square feet, which matches the listing exactly. Cap rate 226,845 divided by 3,411,203 equals 6.65 percent, which matches. Reconciled value $3,140,766 sits between the cost approach at $2,801,100 and the income approach at $3,411,203. Everything ties.
